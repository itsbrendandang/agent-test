# The revision
You wrote a draft. Two reviewers on a different vendor's models then looked at all of it:
- The statement checker compared every cited sentence with the source it cites. Its results are under "Statement checks".
- The critic read the whole draft against the sources. Its notes are under "Critic's notes".
Revise the draft once. This is the text the reader gets. A program compares your revision with the draft: a sentence that failed its check is never shown as holding up, and every cited sentence you change or add is checked again.

You must:
- For every sentence marked "not supported" or "contradicted": remove it; or rewrite it so that it says only what the cited source says; or, when it matters for the answer, say plainly and with no marker that the sources found do not show it. For "contradicted", report what the source actually says. Never keep a failed sentence as it is, never keep its claim by dropping the marker, and never move its marker to a source that does not state it.
- A sentence marked "not checked" is one whose check did not run. Leave it exactly as it is: the reader is shown that it was not checked.
- For every critic note: fix it when the sources support the fix (soften the wording, add the caveat, name the kind of source, show both sides). When a note asks for something no source here states, do not invent it: add it to the "Not found in these sources:" paragraph.
- Keep every sentence that passed its check exactly as it is, word for word, unless a critic note is about that sentence. A changed sentence loses its check.
- It is fine for the revised answer to be shorter and to say "not found" or "not enough evidence". Do not replace a removed sentence with a guess.
- Say each thing once. When you add or change a sentence, do not repeat words that a kept sentence next to it already says; join them or leave the repeat out.
- Keep the direct answer first. When the review took away what it rested on, change it to fit what is left, down to "The sources found do not answer this."
- Every rule you wrote the draft under still holds: only the numbered sources, exact numbers, markers at the end of the sentence, no advice.

After the answer, write the `NEXT STEPS` lines as before, for the revised answer. Then, on a new line, write the line `REVIEW NOTES` and then one line for each critic note: `<note number>: addressed` or `<note number>: not addressed: <the reason in a few words>`. A program reads these lines and removes them from the answer. When there are no critic notes, write `REVIEW NOTES` and nothing after it.
